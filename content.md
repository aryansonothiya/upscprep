## 📝 The Hindu Editorial Analysis
*   **Topic:** The RBI's Growing Fiscal Role and its Implications for Fiscal Federalism
*   **Context:** The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) recently approved a record ₹2.87 lakh crore surplus transfer to the Union government for FY26, raising discussions about its evolving role and independence.
*   **Key Takeaways:**
    *   The **record surplus transfer** (₹2.87 lakh crore) provides significant fiscal space to the government without new taxes or borrowing, but also highlights the increasing "fiscalisation" of the RBI's role.
    *   This trend raises concerns about **central bank independence**, as the RBI's activities, primarily for monetary and financial stability, are producing substantial fiscal revenues for the sovereign.
    *   The transfers are **non-tax revenue** and not part of the divisible pool of income tax collections or GST revenues, which are typically subject to Finance Commission formulas and shared with States. This contributes to **fiscal centralisation**.
    *   As surplus transfers become larger and fiscal pressures intensify, maintaining the **institutional distance** of the central bank from governmental fiscal compulsions becomes more challenging and yet, more crucial.

## 🌍 Geography & Environment
*   **Current Issue:** Transboundary Water Resources Management and Environmental Impact
*   **Main Points:**
    *   **Padma Barrage Project (Bangladesh):** Bangladesh has signed off on a new **Padma barrage** (2.1-km, estimated cost ₹39,170 crore) 180 km downstream of India's Farakka barrage. This aims to control the Padma river and mitigate seasonal water scarcity for 6.5 crore people in southwestern and northern Bangladesh.
    *   **Transboundary River Disputes:** The project highlights ongoing **India-Bangladesh water-sharing challenges**, particularly concerning the 1996 Ganges Water Treaty (expiring December 2026) and other unsigned agreements like the Teesta River Treaty.
    *   **Ecological Consequences:** Experts warn of significant **adverse environmental impacts**, similar to those caused by the Farakka barrage, including altered river ecology, reduced groundwater recharge, increased salinity (affecting Sundarbans mangrove forests), and impact on livelihoods of fisherfolk due to changes in sediment flow.
    *   **Alternative Approaches:** Experts suggest that instead of a large barrage, a series of smaller check-dams might be a more environmentally sound approach, though this would require greater cross-country planning and maintenance.
